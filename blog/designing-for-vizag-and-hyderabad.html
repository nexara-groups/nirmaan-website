What Changes When You Design for Vizag Instead of Hyderabad

Two cities, four hundred kilometres apart, and very different buildings

Architecture By Sai Nirmaan Architects 7 min read

We keep offices in both Visakhapatnam and Hyderabad, and we are often asked whether that is really necessary. The cities are in neighbouring states, four hundred kilometres apart, and both are growing fast. Surely a house is a house.

It is not. A design that performs well in Tellapur will underperform in Madhurawada, and the reverse is just as true. The differences are not stylistic. They come from climate, soil, water and what the local trades are good at.

The air is the biggest difference

Visakhapatnam sits on the coast. The air carries salt, and salt is relentless. It finds exposed reinforcement, window hardware, railing fixings, air-conditioner coils and light fittings. A balcony railing specified without thought will show rust within two years in Vizag and will still look fine after ten in Hyderabad.

This drives real decisions. Cover to reinforcement goes up near the coast. Hardware moves to stainless of a grade that costs more than most clients expect. Aluminium gets a heavier anodising or a powder coat rather than a mill finish. Condenser units get located where they are not taking a direct salt-laden wind off the sea. None of this is visible in a rendering, and all of it decides whether the building looks neglected at year five.

Hyderabad has the opposite problem. The air is dry for most of the year and the issue is heat rather than corrosion. West-facing glass that would be merely warm in Vizag becomes punishing on the Deccan plateau, where summer afternoons run several degrees hotter and the nights stay hot too. Shading devices that look decorative on a Hyderabad elevation are usually doing hard work.

Humidity changes what you can specify

Coastal humidity in Visakhapatnam sits high for much of the year. That rules out materials that behave perfectly well inland. Certain engineered wood products swell. Some adhesives fail. Wardrobes built tight against an external wall grow mould on the back panel, which the family discovers only when they move the furniture.

So in Vizag we detail for air movement in places nobody looks: a gap behind built-in storage on external walls, cross ventilation in bathrooms that is real rather than nominal, floor finishes that tolerate being damp. In Hyderabad those details are less critical, and the budget is better spent on insulation and shading.

What the ground is doing

Hyderabad sits largely on granite. Much of the time that is good news, because the bearing capacity is excellent and foundations can be shallow and cheap. The bad news arrives when the rock is at an awkward depth and the site needs breaking, which is slow and noisy and never costs what the first estimate said.

"The layout and the character can travel between cities. The envelope, the specification and the foundation almost never do."

Coastal Andhra is more varied. Sites near the shoreline can have a high water table and poorer soil, which pushes foundations deeper and sometimes to piles. A client who has built in Hyderabad and expects the same substructure cost in Vizag is often surprised, and it is better to surprise them at the feasibility stage than after the excavation has started.

Cyclones are a design case, not a worry

The Andhra coast takes cyclones. Hudhud in 2014 is still the reference point in Visakhapatnam, and anyone who was there remembers what it did to roofs, glazing and trees.

Designing for that is ordinary engineering rather than anything heroic. Wind loads get taken seriously rather than assumed. Parapets and canopies, which are the first things to fail, get designed as structure rather than as trim. Large glazed openings on exposed elevations are either reduced or specified to take the pressure. Rooftop equipment gets fixed down properly. Trees near the building are chosen with some thought about what happens when they come over.

Hyderabad has no equivalent case. It has a different one: intense short monsoon downpours onto hard ground, which means drainage and water harvesting need more attention than a coastal site where the soil absorbs more.

Water, and what it does to everything

Hyderabad water is hard. It scales geysers, clogs aerators, marks sanitaryware and shortens the life of taps. Specifying premium fittings without a softening strategy is money poured away, and plenty of Hyderabad homeowners have learned that the expensive way.

Vizag water is generally softer, and the municipal supply is reasonably reliable in most developed areas. Storage requirements differ accordingly, which changes tank sizing, pump selection and sometimes the structure that carries them.

The trades are different too

This is the factor nobody writes about and every builder knows. Hyderabad has deep stone-working capability, because the city has worked with granite for a very long time. Detailed stone cladding and complicated stone flooring are routine there and the pricing reflects that.

Visakhapatnam has strong concrete and marine-influenced construction trades, for the obvious reason that the port and the shipyard have been training them for decades. Complex in-situ concrete work tends to go more smoothly in Vizag than an equally complex stone package would.

Designing against the local skill base produces a building that is expensive and a site that runs late. Designing with it produces better work for less money. This is a large part of why we keep both offices rather than running everything from one.

Moving a design between the cities

Clients sometimes bring us a plan they liked from the other city and ask us to build it here. We will, but not unchanged. The layout and the character can travel. The envelope, the material specification, the foundation and the services almost never do.

The house that results looks like the one they fell in love with. It is detailed for where it actually stands.

Building in Visakhapatnam or Hyderabad?

We run studios in both cities and detail each project for the climate, soil and trades it will actually be built in.
